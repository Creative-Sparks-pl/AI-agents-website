## Executive Summary

**For the package as sold today (one price, scripts mandatory in IDE/CLI, key-unlocked server), 12% is the rate every source treats as the cautious choice that survives an audit; no source found a KIS ruling or court judgment on AI skills or prompt packs, so every rate rests on analogy.** [Divergence: pplx and youcom say 12% is the most probable classification for the undivided package [[1, 2, 3, 4]]; claude says 8.5% is the best-documented rate under the letter of the law (MEDIUM) and 12% the likely counter-classification an auditor would apply [[5, 6]]; exa says the most defensible answer is splitting revenue by actual supplies, with 12% as the cautious single rate]

- **8.5%** holds for the Markdown core alone (residual service) and for the server part if it is classified as online software 58.29.40.0; for the whole current product it is MEDIUM–LOW and should not be used without a GUS opinion and an individual ruling [[5, 7, 8]].
- **12%** has the strongest practical support: executable, partly mandatory code delivered by one-time download fits 58.29.2 / ex 58.29.3, yet no rule says any code turns the whole package into software [[1, 2, 3]].
- **15%** does not follow from the word "licence"; it needs a real software-licensing service (58.29.50.0) or data processing (ex 63.11.1), and KIS has declined automatic 15% for e-book and book licences [[9, 10]]. One source adds a separate 15% route for the text core as author/literary services (dział 90) [[11]].
- **5.5%** (production activity) is what KIS applies to authors' own e-books; whether it carries over to a package with code, user tiers and a server is contested [[12, 13]].
- **Most defensible structure:** genuinely separate offerings (content, scripts, server access) with their own prices and records under art. 12 ust. 3; a paper split of one indivisible product can be challenged [[1]].
- **File for a ruling:** all four sources recommend a GUS classification opinion followed by an ORD-IN application (40 zł per state of facts, three-month deadline) that describes the mandatory scripts and the server key in full [[14, 15, 16, 17]]. Align the terms of sale first: they say the product is not software or an online service, while the key unlocks a server the terms never mention.
- PKWiU 2015 remains the reference for ryczałt until 31.12.2028 [[18, 19]].

## §1 Legal Framework

### §1.1 What decides the rate

The rate follows the actual type of activity and, for most specific rates, its PKWiU grouping — not the PKD code, the trade name or the label on the contract or invoice [[1, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25]].

- KIS formula: whether ryczałt applies and at what rate "depends solely on the actual type of services"; each activity must be assigned to a specific PKWiU grouping (HIGH) [[25]].
- Rate structure after Polski Ład, as cited in rulings: pkt 1 – 17%, pkt 2 – 15%, pkt 2a – 14%, pkt 2b – 12%, pkt 3 – 10%, pkt 4 – 12.5% [[26]].
- Consolidated text cited: Dz.U. 2025 poz. 843 [[1, 20]]; one source works from the 2024 consolidated text, Dz.U. 2024 poz. 776 [[22, 23, 24]].

### §1.2 Groupings by rate

|---|---|---|
| 15% | art. 12 ust. 1 pkt 2 lit. e | licensing: software 58.29.50.0; books 58.11.60.0; other printed matter 58.19.30.0 [[1, 22, 27, 28]] |
| 15% | art. 12 ust. 1 pkt 2 lit. i | data processing ex 63.11.1; other information services 63.99 [[1]] |
| 12% | art. 12 ust. 1 pkt 2b lit. a | system (58.29.1) and application (58.29.2) software packages; downloadable software ex 58.29.3 [[1, 22, 29, 30, 31]] |
| 12% | art. 12 ust. 1 pkt 2b lit. b | software-related services ex 62.01.1; software originals 62.01.2 [[1, 29, 32]] |
| 8.5% | art. 12 ust. 1 pkt 5 lit. a | residual: services not covered elsewhere [[1, 22, 33, 34]] |

| 5.5% | production activity, art. 4 ust. 1 pkt 4 | new products, incl. sale of own-made products [[12, 13]] |

Exact wording that carries the analysis:

- 12% lit. a ends „z wyłączeniem pobierania oprogramowania w trybie on-line” [[1, 31]].
- 58.29.50.0 reads „Usługi licencyjne związane z nabywaniem praw do korzystania z programów komputerowych” [[27, 37, 38]].
- PKWiU 2015 lists 58.29.40.0 „Oprogramowanie komputerowe on-line” separately; art. 12 does not name it [[3, 5, 39]].
- 58.29.32.0 reads „Oprogramowanie użytkowe pobierane z Internetu” [[40]].
- 8.5% is residual: it applies only when no specific rate covers the activity, so it does not mean every digital-content sale is 8.5% [[33, 35]].
- The education rate requires the seller to provide an education service; educational content in a ready product is not enough (HIGH) [[22, 36]].

### §1.3 Art. 12 ust. 3: several activities, several rates

Use separate rates only for genuinely separate supplies whose revenue the records attribute to each activity [[1, 26, 41, 42]].

- The rule does not allow splitting the price of one indivisible sale just to reach a lower rate; the split must show in the contract, price list, invoices, terms and books [[1]]. KIS warns against artificially dividing one service [VENDOR: ifirma] [[43]].
- When records cannot attribute revenue, the highest rate among the activities applies [VENDOR: Poradnik Przedsiębiorcy] [[44]].
- Two sources flag the 2025 wording of art. 12 ust. 3 as not confirmed against ISAP [UNVERIFIED].

### §1.4 Who classifies: taxpayer, GUS, KIS

The taxpayer classifies; KIS takes the applicant's PKWiU as given; GUS information is evidence, not a binding decision.

- PKWiU 2015 classifies by character, purpose, technology and mode of supply, and separates goods (incl. software packages) from services (incl. licensing services) [[37, 45]].
- Classification goes to the most detailed grouping; the producer or service provider is responsible because it knows the product; when in doubt, ask the competent GUS unit (HIGH) [[46]].
- KIS generally adopts the applicant's PKWiU and does not classify; in an online-courses ruling it stated it took the applicant's 85.59.19.0 without deciding its correctness (HIGH) [[36]]. The applicant gives the PKWiU and the authority does not verify it [[47]]. In practice KIS often expects a symbol and treats it as part of the facts [[48, 49]].
- Individual rulings bind only the applicant; GUS information is not generally binding law [[50, 51]].
- NSA II FSK 487/23 (26.01.2024): the authority may not make a ruling conditional on the taxpayer stating a PKWiU code when the question is the tax effect of activities described in detail [[50, 52, 53]]. The judgment does not remove the practical need to present a classification when the provision depends on one [[50, 51]].

### §1.5 PKWiU 2025

PKWiU 2025 took effect on 1.1.2026, but income taxes, including ryczałt, keep PKWiU 2015 until 31.12.2028; describe the product and any GUS request in PKWiU 2015 terms (HIGH) [[18, 19, 54, 55]].

- For VAT, PKWiU 2015 applies until the end of 2027 [[19]].
- PKWiU 2025 would matter for ryczałt from 2029 only if art. 12 is amended (SPECULATIVE).

## §2 Position A — 8.5%

### §2.1 Strongest version

The seller sells neither a programming service, a standalone application nor a SaaS platform: it delivers an authored set of instructions, procedures and knowledge (about 140 Markdown files, 1.9 MB) that the client uploads into an external AI model, which does the design work; with no specific grouping, that is residual service activity at 8.5% [[1, 33]].

- Commercial value lies in the content: UX frameworks, audit procedures, 16 site types; .md files are not executable, and their "executor" is a language model reading natural language.
- The code is a set of AI helpers: not sold separately, no human user interface, no installation by the client; in web chats the product works without it.
- If the product falls outside 58.29, 58.11/58.19 and dział 90, the residual rate applies; KIS has taxed content licences with no specific art. 12 heading as 8.5% services: 3D-model licences (21.04.2026; LOW, only a fragment of the ruling visible) and music licences [VENDOR: Poradnik Przedsiębiorcy] (§5.6) [[56, 57]].
- Some digital publications stay at 8.5%: paid online publications in 58.19.21.0 (§5.1) [[58]].
- No direct ruling says whether Markdown used as AI instructions is an electronic publication.

### §2.2 Rulings applying the residual rule to digital and AI services

KIS lets services outside the 12% groupings take 8.5% but checks the actual nature of the work (§5.4, §5.5).

- AI model development, 62.09.20.0: 8.5%, because the work was neither software installation nor software-related services (MEDIUM/HIGH) [[59]].
- Operational, organisational, training and relationship work around a ready platform, 74.90.20.0: 8.5%, provided no software-related services, programming advice or systems management are actually performed (MEDIUM/HIGH) [[60]].
- These are analogies to the residual principle, not rulings on a Markdown package.
- [Divergence: youcom cites 2022 rulings giving 8.5% for designing interfaces and IT systems and for website design as support for 8.5% [[61, 62]]; claude says the line has tightened since 2023 and 2026 rulings give 12% to UX/UI designers who write no code, because the link to software may be indirect [[63]]]

### §2.3 Online software (58.29.40.0)

58.29.40.0 is not named in art. 12 and ex 58.29.3 expressly excludes online download, so KIS consistently accepts 8.5% for it; the route fits mainly the server component (§5.2) [[5, 7, 8, 47, 64]].

- 0112-KDSL1-1.4011.123.2023.2.MJ: revenue classified as 58.29.40, remote online software, falls outside 12% [[7]]. [Divergence: pplx says KIS confirmed 8.5% there [[7]]; claude says only the distinction between software downloaded to the client's computer and software "downloaded online" and the applicant's 8.5% position were readable, the ruling itself unverified [[65]]]
- 0113-KDIPT2-1.4011.703.2023.2.ID: online sale of access to, and updates of, plugin and template files, perpetual or by subscription, classified by the applicant as 58.29.40.0 — position correct, 8.5% [[5, 8]].
- 0112-KDSL1-2.4011.481.2023.3.AP: plugins sold "in code form" by subscription with support and updates, 58.29.40.0 (8.5% per title; full ruling unverified) [[6]].
- SaaS in 58.29.40.0: 8.5% [[64]]; a software subscription gets 8.5% only if the service really has that character [[47]].
- [Divergence: claude says the 2023 plugin-and-template rulings are the closest analogy to a skill with code [[5, 6]]; pplx, youcom and exa found no confirmed ruling on WordPress plugins or themes, and pplx describes 703.2023.ID only as services the applicant classified as 58.29.40.0 [[8]]]

Limits of the route:

- The rulings concern services whose essence was remote access to software; they do not settle an API or headless-browser call returning a site map, nor a text package with built-in access [[7, 66]].
- In 703.2023 and 481.2023 the authority started from the applicant's 58.29.40.0 and did not test it; protection covers only the described facts [[47]].
- In 123.2023, online mode means using an application on a remote server through a browser; a .zip downloaded once points to ex 58.29.3, so the argument covers mainly the server part [[65]].

### §2.4 Education (dział 85)

Do not rest the package on the education rate: dział 85 covers a teaching process for people, and the skill instructs an AI model (LOW).

- Dział 85 covers education services by schools, teachers, instructors or training providers; handing over material is not in itself an education service [[36]].
- KIS applied 8.5% under dział 85 to real teaching, workshops, courses and webinars, e.g. 85.59.19.0 in 0115-KDST2-1.4011.625.2024.2.NC [[12]]; online courses and recorded educational videos [[67]]; trainings [[68]]; AI trainings [[69]]; premium access to online educational content, where the subject is educational content rather than software or SaaS [[70]] (§5.5).
- Dział 85 becomes arguable only if the seller adds a training, course, instruction, consultations, assessment of work or an education platform.

### §2.5 Code as an ancillary element

The ancillary-code argument has some force but no ryczałt ruling backs it, and mandatory use in IDE/CLI cuts against it (SPECULATIVE/MEDIUM).

- For: the code is not offered separately, saves and validates results, is no standalone application, works only inside the UX process, is small next to the Markdown (about 9% of volume), and the client buys knowledge, not programming functions.
- Against: ryczałt has no general rule that an ancillary element takes the main supply's rate; art. 12 ust. 3 assumes separate revenue types [[41, 42]]. KIS does not let one supply be split artificially, and the argument cuts both ways: treated as one supply, the code (mandatory in IDE) may be deemed its characteristic element [[43]].
- Volume share is not a statutory or PKWiU test; function, the buyer's purpose, mode of use and economic independence of the parts decide (MEDIUM as a risk).
- Composite-supply criteria — whether additions are ends in themselves or only enable the main supply, and whether separating them would be economically artificial — appear in administrative sources mostly for other taxes; do not transfer the VAT test mechanically [[71, 72]].

Ratings: §7.1. 5.5% as a lower alternative: §6.1.

## §3 Position B — 12%

### §3.1 Strongest version

The package is a functional set of instructions, code and templates delivered by one-time download, which fits 58.29.2 / ex 58.29.3 under art. 12 ust. 1 pkt 2b lit. a (HIGH on the provision, MEDIUM on its application) [[1, 3, 4, 22, 23, 24]].

- Executable code: `ux_utils.py` (about 4,100 lines, 9 CLI commands), a JS capture script, about 336 KB of browser JavaScript in the HTML templates; versioning (v4.5) and checksums.
- The code performs product functions: saving results, validation that blocks incomplete AI work, summaries for the AI, fetching the site map from the server.
- In IDE/CLI environments saving results and quality checks are mandatory; the client never runs the scripts, but the AI runs them as part of the workflow.
- Delivery is a one-time download, so the online exclusion does not apply; candidates are 58.29.32.0 „Oprogramowanie użytkowe pobierane z Internetu” [[2, 40]] and 58.29.29.0, packages of other application software, if GUS sees the files as a functional application-software set.
- A GUS Łódź opinion placed products in 58.29.32.0 in IBPB-1-1/4511-67/16, under the old 17% rate; the rate is now 12% (MEDIUM) [[73]].
- [Divergence: youcom lists ex 62.01.1 software-related services (12%) as possible where the supply is enabling functionality, maintaining components, updates or server integration; pplx says the 12% route is publishing (58.29.2 / ex 58.29.3), not ex 62.01.1, because a ready package is sold; claude says the 62.01.1 line concerns services, so applying it to a package is analogy [[74]]]

### §3.2 The "related to software" line

KIS and the courts read "services related to software" broadly, but that line concerns services (ex 62.01.1), not the sale of a ready product; use it as a signal of the authority's stance, not as a rule for this package [[74, 75, 76]].

- NSA II FSK 622/24 (23.08.2024): activities connected with software can take 12% even when the taxpayer does not only program; WSA I SA/Gl 670/23 also backed the broad reading [[74, 75, 76]].
- 0115-KDST2-2.4011.318.2024.3.BM: 8.5% refused where the applicant gave 62.01.12.0 and confirmed the link to software [[77]].
- Eureka 703921 (0115-KDST2-2.4011.446.2026.3.KK): the link to software may be direct or indirect, and no programming does not exclude 12% [[78]]; Eureka 691585: 12% for design, writing source code, building and modifying modules, 62.01.11.0 [[79]]; Eureka 546472: 12% for 62.01.12.0 [[80]].
- 2026 rulings gave 12% to UX/UI designers who do not code (MEDIUM; signatures from a commentary, full texts unread) [COMMENTARY: eres.pl] [[63]].
- Counter-line: 8.5% for technical support 62.02.30.0 not tied to software consulting [[81, 82, 83]].

### §3.3 Does code make the whole package software?

Not automatically: no source found a rule that a file containing code is software publishing, and art. 74 copyright gives no "code = software" tax test [[84]].

- Art. 74 protects programs in every form of expression — source code, object code and design materials that allow the program to be recreated — and excludes the ideas and principles behind it [[84, 85, 86]].
- KIS and NSA have not applied art. 74 to a comparable product [UNVERIFIED as a negative search]. One source says the Python and JS scripts are "without doubt" programs under art. 74, with no ryczałt ruling citing it [UNVERIFIED].
- Deciding factors: what the code does; whether the product needs it; functional independence; how it is sold; purpose for the user; relation to the other parts; how payment is set.
- Here the code is no mere example: it performs real operations and part of quality control is mandatory, which supports treating it as a significant usable component.
- A prompt pack with one macro, a Notion template or an Excel file with an incidental script can differ from a program.
- An auditor could treat the Markdown as the "program description and auxiliary materials" of software, making the whole a software package with documentation (SPECULATIVE) [[87]].
- No ruling says whether code run by an AI assistant, not a person, changes the analysis.

Critique of 12% for the whole:

- Code is about 9% of volume; the web version works without it; the code does not perform UX functions, it only saves and validates AI output; the .md files are natural-language instructions for a model.
- No interpretation or judgment has treated a prompt pack or skill as software.
- Volume supports the ancillary argument, while mandatory use supports the opposite; the ratio alone answers nothing.

### §3.4 Downloaded vs online

KIS separates software downloaded and run on the client's device (12%) from software used remotely on the provider's server (8.5% as 58.29.40.0); describe the downloaded package and the server function as two separate facts [[2, 7]].

- 0112-KDIL2-2.4011.788.2026.2.MG: an app downloaded from a platform and run locally, 58.29.32.0, 12%, with GUS opinions cited; the description refers back to 123.2023.MJ (remote-only program, 58.29.40, 8.5%) [[2, 7]].
- The exclusion targets cases where the user gets no lasting file and uses a program running in the provider's environment; PKWiU separates downloadable software (58.29.3) from online software (58.29.40.0) [[3, 39]].
- The exclusion supports keeping SaaS and continuous access out of this part of the 12%, but it does not answer whether the downloaded package is software at all.
- App rulings: in 0113-KDIPT2-1.4011.165.2023.2.ID the applicant cited 0114-KDIP3-62.4011.1203.2021.2.MR confirming 12% for app licences [[88]]; KIS gave 12% for unlocking premium functions in apps (software publishing) and 8.5% for ad space [[89]].
- 0113-KDIPT2-1.4011.755.2025.2.MAP (11.12.2025) cites NSA II FSK 487/23 [[31]]. [Divergence: youcom, via a secondary description, reads it as holding that software-related services for apps downloaded online fall within the exclusion and may take 8.5%, not 12% [[31]]; claude reads it as rejecting the applicant's 5.5% for transferring app rights (position partly incorrect), details unverified [[31]]]
- Fallback if the package is held to be software: argue online software (58.29.40). Weak for a one-time ZIP, because in 703.2023 the applicant herself described the file access as online.

### §3.5 The no-code chat version

The web-chat version without scripts helps the 8.5% argument but does not remove the 12% risk if it is only a "lighter mode" of one product.

- Defend separate classification only if the no-code version is a genuinely separate product: own name, own price, own description, no code, no server access.

Ratings: §7.1.

## §4 Position C — 15%

### §4.1 Strongest version

The client buys a right of use, not files: Solo, Studio and Agencja tiers by user count, a redistribution ban, six months of updates and a licence key to the server; if the subject is a computer program, that is a 58.29.50.0 licensing service at 15% [[1, 22, 27, 28, 90]].

- Terms of sale: „Kupujesz licencję na użytkowanie, nie prawa autorskie do plików”.
- Seat and redistribution limits are typical of software licences and may weigh more than delivery as a file.
- WSA Kraków I SA/Kr 583/15: a licence is a paid right to use an application for a period; one-off payment does not rule out a licence [[91]].
- NSA II FSK 245/11 and II FSK 2677/13: a licence may cover use, modification, distribution or sublicensing, and the contractual scope shapes the supply [[92, 93]].

### §4.2 Why the licence wording does not decide

15% needs two findings — the subject is a computer program, and the transaction is a licensing service in 58.29.50.0; the word "licence" establishes neither (HIGH on the principle) [[1, 2, 25]].

- The client may simply receive a copy for its own use with no right to exploit economic copyright; then the publishing groupings (58.29.2 / ex 58.29.3) are the ones to test [[1, 2]].
- 58.29.50.0 is specific to programs; separate licensing groupings exist for books (58.11.60.0) and other publications (58.19.30.0) [[27]].
- "Kupujesz licencję, nie prawa autorskie" may even weaken the claim that a program was sold, but does not say whether the licence covers software, text or both.
- Renaming "licence" to "sale of a file" will not lower the rate if the client in fact gets only a limited right of use; nor does the word "licence" turn the sale of a copy into a licensing service.
- [Divergence: exa, via ePorady24 commentary, says a time-limited, perpetual or seat-limited licence can lead to 15% when the client economically obtains a right to use the program [COMMENTARY: ePorady24] [[94]]; claude, via a vendor blog, says 58.29.50.0 covers rights to create and distribute software and to use its components when building other programs — publisher-type licences, not an end-user licence to one copy, which belongs to 58.29.1–58.29.3 (MEDIUM; full GUS explanations unread) [VENDOR: Taxe.pl] [[87]]]
- No court judgment was found that separates 58.29.50.0 from 58.29.2 / ex 58.29.3 for an identical download-plus-licence sale; I SA/Po 6/23, III SA/Wa 1941/19 and III SA/Wa 1848/25 do not decide this question [[95, 96, 97]].

### §4.3 Content licences: e-books and books

KIS has not applied 15% just because a content contract is called a licence (§5.1) [[9, 10, 25]].

- 0112-KDSL1-2.4011.372.2025.2.PS (14.08.2025): an author's non-exclusive licence to her e-book for a company's internal platform, applicant's 58.11.30.0 — taxed as lease-like income, 8.5% up to 100,000 zł and 12.5% above, not 15% and not 5.5% [[9]].
- 0115-KDIT1.4011.161.2022.3.MN: 15% for licences to sell books held incorrect although the applicant gave 58.11.60.0 [[10]].
- 0112-KDSL1-2.4011.133.2025.2.MO (Eureka 636270): classification of translating and selling a book follows the actual subject and can lead to a publishing grouping, not a licensing one [[25]].
- Author's own e-books sold by the author took 5.5%, not 15% (257.2023.MO, Eureka 692511) [[13, 98]].
- Counter-risk: if the core is a "publication" close to an e-book, a licence to it could be licensing of rights to books (58.11.60.0), 15% [[25]].

### §4.4 Author and literary services (dział 90)

Dział 90 is the most serious 15% route for the text core, though it fits commissioned creative work rather than selling copies of a finished product (raised by one source only).

- Dział 90 cultural and entertainment services are taxed 15%; rulings applied 15% to preparing educational materials [VENDOR: Poradnik Przedsiębiorcy] [[99]].
- Ruling of 30.05.2025 (signature unread): the applicant itself placed creating text-based educational materials in 90.03.11 „Usługi świadczone przez autorów, kompozytorów, rzeźbiarzy i pozostałych artystów, z wyłączeniem artystów wykonawców” at 15% [[11]].
- 0114-KDIP3-2.4011.68.2022.2.MG: 15% for writing texts as literary creative activity [[100]].
- Critique: dział 90 covers a creator's services; selling mass-copied units of a finished product is publishing (dział 58) or production, as the 5.5% e-book line shows [[101]].

### §4.5 When 15% strengthens or weakens

- Stronger when: the code is a standalone program; the client gets a right to use it; the key activates a program or service; the fee depends on user count; the licence covers time, seats, an organisation or a scope of use; use without a licence is prohibited.
- Weaker when: the licence only bars resale of text files and the main subject is content and instructions.
- Data processing (ex 63.11.1) for the server: §6.2.

Ratings: §7.1.

## §6 Other Possible Classifications

### §6.1 5.5% — production activity

KIS applies 5.5% to authors who create and sell their own e-books, so an authored digital file is not automatically an 8.5% service; whether that reaches a package with code, user tiers and a server is unresolved (§5.1) [[12, 13, 98, 101, 102, 106]].

- Art. 4 ust. 1 pkt 4 defines production activity as activity producing new products, including the sale of own-made products [[13]].
- Distinction: in 372.2025.PS KIS separated selling a created work from paid licensing and applied the lease rule, not 5.5%, to the licence [[9]].
- Against: 3D-model licences and similar treated as a service, not production [[56]]; templates, visualisations and 3D models sold on a platform denied 5.5% [[104]]; 5.5% refused for app rights in 755.2025 [[31]]; for software sold over the internet, the absence of goods excludes 5.5% and 3% [COMMENTARY: eporady24 – T. Krywan] [[94]].
- The 12% software-package or 15% licence headings may be treated as specific provisions overriding the production category.
- Use 5.5% only after confirming the client buys a copy of an own product, not a licensing service, software or an online service.
- Removing code and the server would raise 5.5%'s relevance for the text core (§7.3).
- [Divergence: pplx, exa and claude treat 5.5% as an open alternative grounded in the e-book line [[12, 13, 98, 102]]; youcom says there is no basis for 5.5% because selling a digital package is not production activity, even if the author made the content and code]

- Making application software available without data processing: 8.5% for 63.11.13.0 per the summary of 0115-KDST2-2.4011.57.2026.2.BM [[103]].
- Online software 58.29.40.0 at 8.5%: §2.3.
- It could also be a technical or software-related service; no ruling covers a comparable headless-browser service.
- [Divergence: youcom says the service probably takes 15% as data processing (LOW–SPECULATIVE), possibly 8.5% or 12%; claude says it is a SaaS-type element best defended at 8.5% under 58.29.40.0, with a small 63.11.1 risk [[5, 64]]; pplx and exa say it depends on classification: 8.5% as online software 58.29.40.0 or as 63.11.13.0, or 15% as data processing ex 63.11.1 [[7, 103, 107]]]

### §7.2 Decisive Product Facts

1. **Mandatory code in IDE/CLI** pushes hardest toward 12%: saving and quality checks cannot run without it.
2. **Dominant function and value.** If the client mainly buys the knowledge base and procedures and the scripts help, publication, residual service or production gains; if the main result is a working workflow whose key functions the scripts perform, software and 12% gain.
3. **Works without scripts in web chats** (mobile wireframes only, no completeness checks, template filled by hand) is the main argument that content is the core; it does not decide if one product is sold with several modes. Describe every mode in the application, not only the most favourable.
4. **Delivery.** A one-time archive download is closer to downloadable software (ex 58.29.3, 12%) than to SaaS; remote access points to 58.29.40.0 (8.5%) [[2, 7]].
5. **Server key.** The key unlocks a separate online function the seller maintains, with limits and a free/paid tier; this is a supply beyond handing over a file and can be assessed regardless of the contract label.
6. **Terms of sale.** "The product is not software" does not bind the authority. Its clash with a key-unlocked online service the terms never mention weakens the credibility of the description; that silence is an evidentiary risk, not proof the service is absent.
7. **Licence and per-user tiers** are evidence, not a verdict: software licences may take 15%, while licences to own works have gone to the lease rule [[1, 9]]. [Divergence: claude says per-user licensing matters little because the label does not decide; youcom lists the user-count tiers as pointing to licensed access, and exa says the key strengthens 15%]
8. **Size and proportions.** No statutory threshold for code share exists; 1.9 MB of Markdown against 4,100 Python lines supports content dominance, but 336 KB of template JavaScript and mandatory validation complicate it.
9. **Six months of updates** add an element of continuing service.
10. **Separate purchasability.** Whether each part can be bought on its own, and whether the key can be deactivated, shapes whether there are one or several supplies.

### §7.3 Decision Table

| Scenario | Most defensible rate | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Current package, one price, no split, no ruling | 12% (cautious) | MEDIUM; HIGH that it survives audit |
| Markdown only, no code, no server | 8.5%, possibly 5.5% | MEDIUM; LOW–MEDIUM before a GUS opinion |
| Markdown plus optional example scripts | 8.5%, or 12% per GUS | MEDIUM |
| Markdown plus mandatory Python, JS, interactive templates | 12% | not rated |
| Current ZIP, content and functional helper separated | 12% code; 8.5% content | MEDIUM |
| Current ZIP sold as one right of use | 15% possible on the whole | MEDIUM |
| Own digital product sold as copies, no licensing service | 5.5% may beat 8.5% | MEDIUM for e-books; LOW for skill |
| Standalone downloadable program or toolkit | 12% (58.29.2 / ex 58.29.3) [[1]] | MEDIUM–HIGH |
| Code sold separately at its own price | 12% code; rest per its own class | MEDIUM to MEDIUM/HIGH |
| Scripts and server only as an online service | 8.5% (58.29.40.0) [[7]] | MEDIUM to MEDIUM–HIGH |
| Separate program licence, 58.29.50.0 confirmed | 15% (12% if issuing the program) | HIGH after classification |
| Key-unlocked server as a separate service | 8.5% or 15% per PKWiU (§6.2) | MEDIUM |
| Separate processing of client data, ex 63.11.1 | 15% | MEDIUM |
| Licence of the author's content akin to a lease | 8.5% to 100,000 zł; 12.5% above [[9]] | MEDIUM as analogy |
| One price for files and online service | dominant or most specific element's rate | high risk |
| Separate products, prices and records | separate rates (art. 12 ust. 3) | — |

[Divergence: for the current package without a ruling, claude puts 8.5% under the letter of the law with the authority able to apply 12% (MEDIUM–LOW); pplx, youcom and exa put 12% as the defensible single rate]

Row risks: the Markdown-only variant still carries a 15% risk (content licence or dział 90) and a chance of 5.5%; selling code separately invites an artificial-split challenge if the content is incomplete without the scripts.

**Changes that move the rate.** Make only real changes; an artificial split of one price into "content" and "code" with no separate scope, purchasability and function can be challenged [[1]].

- Toward 8.5% (or 5.5%): remove code from the base package; sell code as a separate, optional add-on; drop mandatory scripts; remove the licence key or the online service; charge separately for training or consultation; offer the product as educational material. Removing code and server strengthens the text layer as a publication or own creative product, which can raise 5.5%'s relevance rather than guarantee 8.5%.
- Toward 12%: make code the core function; extend automation; add an application interface; make use depend on a runtime; sell a functional plugin or extension.
- Selling scripts separately supports a separate rate only with their own description, a real price, purchase possible without the texts, a separate licence and actual separate sales; an invoice line alone does not satisfy art. 12 ust. 3 [[1]].
- Offer the online service as its own plan with its own scope, access terms and price if it has separate value; then test 58.29.40.0 (8.5%) against data processing (15%).
- Selling without user limits and without licence language weakens 15%; dropping the online service removes the 63.11.1 and key questions.
- Bring the terms of sale in line with how the product works; do not delete the server information to reach a lower rate.
- Make IDE/CLI functions optional or a separate module only if that reflects real use, not paperwork prepared for the application.

## §8 Securing the Position

### §8.1 Sequence

GUS classification opinion → ORD-IN application to KIS → records that separate the package, the code and the online service.

### §8.2 GUS classification opinion

Ask the Ośrodek Klasyfikacji i Nomenklatur in Łódź for a classification, stating the purpose (ryczałt); its information is not an administrative decision and does not bind KIS, but KIS cites such opinions and commentators advise getting one before applying [[48, 49, 63, 94, 109]].

- KIS rulings quote GUS opinions, e.g. a 2026 UX ruling: "62.01.11.0 (opinia GUS)" [COMMENTARY: eres.pl] [[63]].
- Describe the product technically, without the marketing words "skill", "asystent AI" or "baza wiedzy", and do not expect GUS to follow the marketing name.
- Include: directory structure; number and type of files; Markdown volume; Python and JavaScript volume; how and by whom the code runs; which scripts are mandatory; environment requirements; what works without code; server functions, inputs, outputs and API; delivery; licence scope; update model; exact entitlements of Solo, Studio and Agencja; how the parts relate; whether each can be bought alone; what the client receives after paying; real files or representative samples [[46, 48]].
- Ask for separate classifications of the Markdown content, the code, the HTML/JS templates and the server service, at least where they are actually offered separately [[46, 48]].
- Put alternative questions: is the package a product or a service; an application-software package; downloadable software; is the online service part of the product or separate; should Markdown and code be classified separately.
- Fee and timing: no source confirmed a fixed GUS fee or a guaranteed deadline [[48]].

### §8.3 Individual ruling (ORD-IN)

File under art. 14b Ordynacji podatkowej: the fee is 40 zł per separate state of facts or future event, and the deadline is three months from a complete application, excluding time spent remedying defects [[14, 15, 16, 17]].

- With many questions and variants, check how the fee is counted. Two sources did not verify the fee and deadline themselves [UNVERIFIED as of 10.11.2026].
- Protection extends only as far as reality matches the description and the taxpayer follows the ruling; a ruling issued on an incomplete or inconsistent description does not protect [[15, 47, 110]]. If the application says "the product is not software" but the described facts show a functional library and a server key, protection may not cover the full model.
- The applicant supplies the PKWiU and KIS does not verify it [[47]]; KIS reserves that a wrong code leaves the consequences of that code unprotected [[12, 50]]. After II FSK 487/23 the application can go in without a code, but for a case this unusual attach the GUS opinion or at least state the proposed groupings [[50, 52, 111]].
- Do not reduce the facts to "these are text files". State: the content and function of each file with approximate volume; whether the client gets a copy of files or access to a system; whether and in which environments the AI runs scripts automatically; what works with and without code; the server's scope, inputs, outputs, usage frequency, limits and free/paid split; pricing, licence plans, licence term and permitted use; whether text and the online service can be bought separately; how records are kept and the price is attributed to parts.
- State the negative facts too: no transfer of rights, no custom programming, no installation by the seller, scripts not required in the chat version, no consultations — and describe exactly what the online service does.
- Ask about the current model and the planned genuinely separate sale as separate events if they differ materially; give alternative positions for 8.5%, 12% and 15%.
